      Step 2 of 7 · Method 1 of 4

      Expert estimation

      Experienced hydrologists can often estimate to within a factor of two just by looking. Make your own estimate before you see anyone else's.

      To do on this step

        Your estimate by eye

        Enter the width, depth and velocity you wrote in your notebook before anyone measured.

        1. Width: pace it out, or compare it with something you know the size of.
        2. Mean depth: usually about two-thirds of the deepest point.
        3. Mean velocity: time a leaf over 2 m, then take about 85% of that speed. Water near the bed moves more slowly than the surface.

        Step 3 of 7 · Shared measurement

        Channel survey

        Float gauging and the slope-area method both need the channel's cross-sectional area A. Slope-area also needs the P. You get both by measuring depths across three .

        To do on this step

          How the transects are surveyed

          1. Stretch a tape tight across the channel, bank to bank, at right angles to the flow.
          2. Record the water's edge on each bank as depth 0 m.
          3. Read the depth every 0.5 m with a metre stick, on its downstream face, so the bow wave doesn't push the reading up.
          4. Repeat at T1 (0 m), T2 (10 m) and T3 (20 m).

          Float gauging

          Time a float over a measured reach to get the surface velocity. The surface moves faster than the water as a whole, so multiply by a , usually 0.8–0.9.

              Step 6 of 7 · Method 4 of 4

              Dilution gauging (salt slug)

              Tip a known mass of dissolved salt into the stream. Downstream, a meter logs the as the salt cloud passes. More water dilutes the salt more, so a smaller area under the curve means a bigger flow.
